One imaginary prompt, two approaches

For “an animal kept as a pet,” a common-answer player might first think of a dog. A rare-answer player might consider a stick insect, then check whether it genuinely fits the wording. These are editorial examples, not confirmed accepted answers or rankings. Randomness is not a substitute for relevance.

Which experience fits you?

  • Choose our preview to inspect a transparent formula and animated platform, while accepting that counts and coverage are simulated.
  • Choose PlayDaily if you want its particular original Noillirk implementation rather than our independent demo.
  • Choose Krillion if finding a less obvious valid response is the part you enjoy. Use its current rules, not a reversed Noillirk score.

Why a finer scale is not automatically a fairer database

Two systems can round differently and still agree about their underlying evidence. Conversely, a detailed 73-point result can look precise while resting on invented counts. The relevant questions are whether an answer is covered, where its value came from, what population was measured and whether the result can be reproduced. Our data inventory makes the remaining limits explicit.

Keep your comparison fair

Record the edition, mode, question version and total possible points before comparing runs. Do not convert height, reward gems or a different version’s multipliers into survey shares. Our local service checks input against a frozen mapping; it cannot validate another site’s answer or overrule its judge.
