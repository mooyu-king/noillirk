THE FIELD NOTES

Your Farflung-inspired game guide.

Need a detail? Open just the chapter you need.
These rules describe our independent edition.

Your first five-stop expedition

Globe Crowd is a small geography game about choosing a country that fits a clue, then deciding whether to trust a familiar answer or pursue a less familiar one. Each expedition has five questions. There is no countdown, no speed bonus and no account requirement. Read at your own pace, inspect the map, and use the country list whenever you need a clearer way to make a selection. The tiny pilot is your personal guide through the flight, rather than a representation of another online player.

Start with Popular if you want the higher positions in our editorial ranking to earn more points. Choose Rare if you want the reverse challenge. Both missions use exactly the same geographic answer sets. The mission changes the scoring direction, not which countries are accepted. Pick a pilot before beginning; the sixteen pilots differ in their face, headwear, hair and flight suit. Their appearance never provides an advantage. After your fifth reveal, open the flight log to see the whole route and copy a result without sharing the answers.

A selection is a choice, not a submission

The question opens on a flat map so you can consider the whole world before committing. Click a supported country shape or search its English name. Two-letter country codes are useful shortcuts: JP finds Japan, BR finds Brazil, and GB finds the United Kingdom. A small alias list also recognises forms such as UK and USA. Search is an alternative to map precision, not a hint system. It lists the supported atlas countries whether or not they are accepted for the current question.

Check the selected country name before pressing Confirm country. Until confirmation, selecting a different map shape or list item simply changes your choice. Once confirmed, the answer is locked and its score is recorded. The game then reveals the country and opens a globe stage: a shared world in Popular, or your own foreground globe in Rare. If you cannot find a country, clear the search and inspect the available list. A missing entry means this edition does not support that country; it is not a statement about its existence or real-world eligibility.

Read the definition before taking off

Geography questions can sound simple while hiding different interpretations. Open What counts? before submitting whenever the wording leaves room for doubt. Region questions follow the grouping attached to our frozen Natural Earth atlas. Land-neighbor questions require a shared land boundary, rather than a ferry route or a short sea crossing. The France neighbor question explicitly refers to metropolitan Europe. Island-country questions allow a country to share an island, and exclude Australia under the definition used in that question.

Marker questions are deliberately different from questions about a whole national territory. A label point north of the Equator does not mean every part of that country lies north of it. Countries may cross the Equator, a meridian or the date line. Read the clue literally and use the supplied definition to know which property is being tested. This makes the map a learning aid instead of asking you to guess an unstated rule. The preview bank is finite and still awaits a separate human release review.

Understand a score between 1.0 and 100.0

Every accepted answer has a position in a frozen editorial order. We turn that position into a number from zero at the first rank to one at the last rank. Popular calculates 100 minus 99 times that number. Rare calculates 1 plus 99 times the same number. Scores are rounded to one decimal place. With five distinct ranked answers, the possible values are 100.0, 75.3, 50.5, 25.8 and 1.0 in the Popular direction. Rare reverses those values.

These values do not describe the percentage of people who chose an answer. They are a game rule applied to an estimated order. The twenty tiers are descriptive labels, not a restriction to twenty score values. A question with fewer answers will naturally leave many tiers unused. Equal editorial weights receive an average tied rank. If a future question has only one accepted answer, either mission awards 100.0 because that question cannot distinguish popularity. Passing, or confirming an atlas country outside the accepted set, earns zero rather than the minimum valid-answer score.

Daily consistency and practice variety

Daily selects five questions using the UTC calendar date and the frozen bank version. Players using the same version on the same UTC date receive the same set in the same order. Popular and Rare share that set, so playing the other mission can reveal information you already know. The game marks subsequent daily starts as familiar or replayed when browser storage is available. There is no public ranking, and a copied local result is not proof of an independently verified score.

Unlimited practice means you can start additional expeditions without a daily limit. It does not mean an infinite question bank. The current preview contains 384 prompts. Practice gives preference to questions not yet selected in the current question-bank cycle on this browser, avoids repeating a question within the same expedition, and spreads selections across available categories. If there are not enough fresh questions, it allows repeats and displays a notice. Clearing browser storage or switching devices removes that local history, so the game cannot guarantee you will never see a familiar clue.

One shared world, or a discovery of your own

The reveal is designed to connect your answer to a place. After confirmation, Popular brings a decorative expedition crew onto one globe. Higher scores invite more of our sixteen distinct pilots, with your pilot in the foreground and a YOU badge. Rare places your pilot alone on the closest globe, while the other fifteen explorers remain in three distant groups. The globes show the region of your selected answer; the country name remains written alongside the score. These figures illustrate the mission, not actual respondent counts or connected players. A passed or unaccepted stop earns no celebration and no Popular companions.

At the end of five stops, the globe stage celebrates your expedition and the written flight log lists all five results. Popular uses your average score to size its decorative crew; Rare keeps your foreground globe private. Confetti is decorative and does not add points. This is a single-player celebration, not a podium claiming that you defeated other connected players. The current renderer uses original illustrated character sprites animated on Canvas with depth shading, rather than imported competitor models or a finished WebGL scene. Each of the sixteen pilots has its own procedural motion sequence: wing waves, double bounces, bows, steps, nods and salutes combine different arm, head and body movements. These are animated two-dimensional sprites, not fully rigged three-dimensional models. Reduce motion freezes the characters and skips the entrance; Skip movement finishes the arrival without changing your answer or score. The result remains readable in ordinary HTML even when the drawing surface is unavailable.

Turn a reveal into a useful geography lesson

Do not rush past a difficult answer. Open Explore answers & source to see the accepted countries and their points for your chosen mission. Clicking an answer in this list reorients the globe toward it without changing the answer you submitted. That distinction is useful: you can explore a neighboring country or an unfamiliar island after scoring, while the recorded result remains intact. The link beside the list identifies the geographic source used for this preview atlas.

The final flight log records every question, country and awarded score. Select a stop to revisit its location on the globe. Compare the original wording with the answer rather than memorising a number in isolation. For example, learning why a marker is west of the prime meridian is more transferable than remembering a particular editorial rank. If you notice an omitted eligible country among the supported choices, report the question wording and country to us. Corrections should produce a new bank version rather than quietly changing the meaning of a completed result.

Choose the right view for your screen

The page keeps the game frame at sixteen by nine so its cover and loaded game occupy the same space. On a desktop, the map and answer panel sit side by side. The toolbar provides fullscreen and a separate-tab option. On a phone, Play in mobile view opens the dedicated game document with the map above the answer panel. This gives country search and the on-screen keyboard more space than a tiny embedded frame. Use Back to guide in the game footer to return to this page.

Use the country list for small islands and tightly packed European borders. You do not need to hit a tiny outline to earn a correct answer. Search with the keyboard, then select an item and confirm. The buttons provide a visible focus indicator, selected country text and pressed states. A simplified map cannot promise survey-level precision, and low-resolution outlines may omit very small details. Search and text results are the dependable alternative when shapes overlap, an outline fails to load, or the drawing performance is poor on your device.

Keep control of movement and your progress

Reduce motion disables floating and confetti and makes rotation immediate. The game initially respects your system preference, and you can change the setting using its header button. Skip movement finishes the stage entrance without skipping the question, deleting an answer or modifying a score. The left and right controls rotate the globe when that view is active. All score explanations and navigation remain available while the decorative movement takes place; you never need to wait for an animation before reading a result.

After a confirmed answer and after moving to the next question, the game attempts to save the run in this browser. Save & return takes you back to the menu, where Resume flight reopens the saved state. A new expedition replaces the current saved run. Storage can be disabled by browser settings or unavailable in some embedded contexts; in that case the status explicitly asks you to keep the tab open. Saves do not sync across devices, and a bank-version change can invalidate an old save so that new questions are not mixed with old scoring rules.

What belongs to this edition

Globe Crowd is an independent geography preview hosted on noillirk.co. It is not the original Farflung game and is not affiliated with Farflung LLC. The original game has its own interface, puzzle selection, accounts and answer decisions. Use the clearly marked original-site link when you want that experience. This page does not provide official daily solutions, import an official question database or submit scores to the original service. The pilots, game rules implementation, guide text and interface here are independently developed.

The map outlines come from Natural Earth public-domain cartography and are simplified for a lightweight game. The playable atlas intentionally includes only 80 selectable countries, while the background shows additional land. Editorial rank is a design choice and may not match your expectations of familiarity. It is neither a population ranking nor evidence from search results or a poll. The preview has no live multiplayer, paid advantage, AI answer judge or public leaderboard. Those boundaries matter when comparing results or deciding which game you actually want to play.

BEFORE YOU FLY

A few good answers.

Random flights, daily clues and what your score really means.

Is this the official Farflung game?

No. This is Globe Crowd, an independently developed geography preview on noillirk.co. It is not affiliated with Farflung LLC. Use the original-site link for the official game.

How large is the current bank?

The preview has 384 questions and 80 selectable countries across ten categories: regions, borders, geography, map reading, area, capitals, names, languages, currencies and coasts. Many clues are generated from explicit geographic conditions. It is not a complete world atlas or a fully human-reviewed release bank.

Are the rankings real player percentages?

No. They are frozen editor-estimated orders. Scores come from rank position, not a measured response rate. The pilot represents your local avatar, not a real crowd.

Why did I get zero when the minimum is one?

The 1.0 minimum applies only to an accepted answer. Passing or submitting a supported country outside this question’s answer set earns zero. Missing countries are not available choices in this atlas.

What happens if I reload?

When storage is available, Resume flight restores the last saved question or reveal for this bank version. An unconfirmed selection is not preserved. A new bank version may invalidate a previous save.

Will Unlimited never repeat questions?

No. It allows repeated expeditions from a finite bank and prioritizes questions not yet selected in the current full-bank cycle on this browser. It warns when repeats are necessary; clearing storage resets that history.

Does Daily use my local midnight?

This independent edition uses midnight UTC. The same date and bank version give the same five questions in either mission. This is separate from the original Farflung schedule.

Can I play with friends in the same room?

Not yet. This version is single-player. Sharing copies a local, unverified summary with no answers; it does not create a server-backed challenge, room or leaderboard entry.

Can I stop the animations?

Yes. Reduce motion disables floating and confetti and makes rotations instant. Skip movement ends only the current rotation. Neither option changes scores.

How do I report a questionable answer?

Use the Contact page and include the question wording, country, bank version and whether you used Popular or Rare. Do not send credentials or private account information.
